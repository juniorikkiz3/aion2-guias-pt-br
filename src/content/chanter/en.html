The Chanter fights in melee with a staff and makes the whole party stronger at the same time: a buffer and a backup healer that every group wants. Don't expect top damage — with equal gear Chanter and Cleric are the two lowest damage dealers — but the party hits harder, dies less and gets its debuffs cleansed.

The build in one sentence

Keep {{ s('undefeated-mantra') }} on and hold the macro on the line where {{ s('spinning-strike') }}, {{ s('marchutans-wrath') }} and {{ s('impactful-crush') }} open {{ s('dark-crush') }} again and again, weaving {{ s('onslaught') }} between every hit.

How Dark Crush works: it can only be used for a short time after a ranged skill — 2 seconds after {{ s('spinning-strike') }} or {{ s('impactful-crush') }}, 3 seconds after {{ s('marchutans-wrath') }}. At skill level 16 its own cooldown disappears, so inside that window you press it again and again. Everything in the build is about keeping that window open.

The PvE mode follows Arthars Gaming's Chanter starter guide for the global launch (video, September 26; eight months on the class on the Taiwan server), read frame by frame. Checked against Grobs' Chanter PvE guide (English, global, October 5).

{% endcall %} {% call section('global', 'Global Season 1: what changes', 'pve pvp') %}

Global servers open on October 5, 2026 (Founder's early access from September 30). What that means for the Chanter:

  • 4 stigma slots, capped at 20. The Chanter has more useful stigmas than slots, so the four you take are a real choice (see Stigmas).
  • Little cooldown reduction and combat speed at the start. Every specialization that shortens a cooldown is worth more, and fights last longer than on Taiwan.
  • Skill level 20 is hard to reach in Season 1. Plan for 16 on the key skills first; 20 comes from arcana later.
  • Bigger groups (5-player expeditions, 10-player sanctuaries): with a Cleric in the party your mantra does not stack with Light of Protection (at the same level yours wins), and Wave Blow's debuff does not stack with the Cleric's Debilitating Mark.
  • PvP: everyone starts on the same limited gear and no meta has formed yet.

Active skills and passives are identical in the global and KR/TW data; stigmas lose their level-25 tier.

{% endcall %} {% call section('gear', 'Gear & stats', 'pve') %}
  • Rings: the only place for active skills — four or five lines (crafted rings have one more) that take the key skills from 14 to 16.
  • Weapon and guard: never put skills there — their damage lines are worth more.
  • Passives come from the other pieces. Global gear also rolls Might, Precision and Combat Speed there, so expect fewer passive lines than on Taiwan.
  • Accuracy is short early on: without it you have to hit bosses from behind and get parried.
  • Manastones: Attack, Critical Hit, Accuracy. Guard (off-hand) to +10.

Mantras are toggles: switch them on once and keep them out of any macro — a second press turns them off. {{ s('barrier-spell') }}, {{ s('ensnaring-mark') }}, and {{ s('assault-shock') }} have no place in PvE. {{ s('fracturing-blow') }} is Grobs' pick for a premade group early in the season: Defense is a flat reduction, so lowering it is worth most while gear is low.

{% endcall %} {% call section('macro', 'Macro & rotation', 'pve') %}

AION 2 has two separate tools:

  • Hotbar line — several skills stacked on one hotbar button. Pressing it casts the highest-priority skill that's ready, and in AION 2 the bottom slot has the highest priority. The cards show each line top to bottom as in the game.
  • In-game macro — while you hold its key, it presses your hotbar buttons in order and cancels animation tails. Skills on cooldown are skipped.

Before Dark Crush reaches 16 its cooldown breaks this line: the macro spends the openers while Dark Crush is still cooling down, and the 3-second window goes to waste. notXeon's fix until then: keep one opener on its own key and press it yourself the moment Dark Crush is ready again.

Another option: Arthars' line

One line with Incandescent Blow in it and {{ s('marchutans-wrath') }} on its own key; the macro presses the line, then Onslaught.

{% call hotline('E', 'Dark Crush line', ['dark-crush', 'spinning-strike', 'impactful-crush', 'incandescent-blow']) %}Dark Crush fires whenever its window is open. When it closes, Spinning Strike opens it again, then Impactful Crush does; Incandescent Blow fills in when both are on cooldown.{% endcall %}

Keep {{ s('marchutans-wrath') }} off this line. On its own key it is your reserve: a way to open Dark Crush when both ranged skills are on cooldown, or right after you had to step away from the boss. On the line it would push Incandescent Blow out of the rotation.

With this line spam Onslaught and the line key yourself as well — your own presses land between the macro's steps; {{ s('focused-defense') }} on big hits.

Where the damage comes from in Arthars' test: Dark Crush and its Piercing Strike follow-up about 40%, then Bursting Blow, Storm Chain, Incandescent Blow and Spinning Strike at about 10% each. That is why Dark Crush goes to 16 first and Onslaught needs Storm Chain.
